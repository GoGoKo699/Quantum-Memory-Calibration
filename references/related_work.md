# Relation to prior work

This project studies the classical information needed to make collective-noise protection accessible when the spatial couplings are unknown. Its model has one uncontrolled storage interval, arbitrary ideal endpoint operations, and no persistent quantum system outside the counted register. Calibration supplies a classical record of the static coupling profile before the payload is encoded. It supplies no record of the realized fluctuating field. The record can be reused while the profile remains fixed.

Two results have different operational roles. For independent interval uncertainty in the couplings, the optimal uncalibrated lifetime at fixed positive rate and fixed error is of order the inverse squared uncertainty width. For a two-valued profile, the optimal vanishing-error storage rate is exactly the available static-profile information per physical qubit, capped at one. The latter has both a hard record-size formulation and a mutual-information formulation. The lifetime result is a fixed-error statement; the exact information frontier takes a vanishing-error limit at fixed nonzero noise. They should not be interchanged.

The comparisons below concern these statements and their assumptions. Bibliographic entries are in [references.bib](../references.bib); primary-source reading limits are recorded in [source_evidence.md](source_evidence.md).

## Protection with a supplied collective-noise model

[Layden, Chen, and Cappellaro (2020)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.124.020504) give efficient error correction for dephasing induced by a common fluctuator. Their construction protects one logical qubit using fixed, well-characterized couplings, with code and recovery choices depending on those couplings. They explicitly discuss sensitivity to miscalibration and the possibility that it limits useful register size. This is a close physical predecessor. The present converse addresses optimization over all endpoint codes at an extensive logical rate, rather than the sensitivity of that particular construction. No numerical robustness claim from their supplemental material is used here.

[Bacon, Lidar, and Whaley (1999)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.60.1944) analyze the stability of specified decoherence-free subspaces under symmetry-breaking perturbations. Their all-orders-in-time memory statement is perturbative in the symmetry breaking; the relevant calculation discards second-order perturbation terms. It does not establish a lifetime upper bound after optimization over all encodings under an independently unknown spatial profile.

[Bartlett, Rudolph, and Spekkens (2003)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.91.027901) show how collective symmetry permits asymptotically efficient communication without a shared reference frame. The group representation is known, while the common group element is unknown. Here the unknown information instead includes an extensive site-dependent embedding of the collective generator. A single fluctuating field therefore need not imply that its protected subspaces are usable without spatial calibration. This distinction preserves the reference-frame result rather than contradicting it.

Two earlier research repositories supply methods and calibrated baselines used here. [Quantum-Spectral-Memory, pinned spectral-law source](https://github.com/GoGoKo699/Quantum-Spectral-Memory/blob/ee5dc0e5529c7775bfaeabb2a5e744c51eb9431d/science/spectral_law.md), supplies the Schur-channel comparison with complete dephasing and the known-spectrum occupancy argument. These are inherited tools. [Inhomogeneous-Collective-Memory, pinned model](https://github.com/GoGoKo699/Inhomogeneous-Collective-Memory/blob/b6bbf3cae33b300d0c793628e168616dc4919bdc/science/model.md), assumes a supplied spatial profile and develops high-rate protection against common fields. Its use of a protected inner block and an outer code informs the achievable construction. The present continuum lower bound is given directly through phase-error correction with a classical linear code.

In particular, the known-profile spectral code is not a new calibration result: bounded supplied couplings allow a large spectral cell and near-unit asymptotic rate at polynomial storage times. The new question concerns what survives when endpoint operations cannot depend freely on that profile.

## Calibration and incomplete noise knowledge

The premise that calibration is an operational memory resource is established prior work. [Danageozian (2023)](https://doi.org/10.22331/q-2023-12-04-1195) derives recovery bounds with incomplete noise knowledge, including parameter estimation using spectator systems and a metrological cost for selecting a recovery. Its illustrative setting includes an approximate amplitude-damping code. It does not optimize a growing register over all endpoint codes with a budget on the retained description of an extensive spatial profile.

[Morales-Hashemi, Smith, and Cao (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03545) demonstrate recovery ambiguity between temporally correlated noise models with identical syndrome statistics in a fixed stabilizer-memory experiment. The final recovery may be an arbitrary channel, but the encoding, extraction procedure, and logical schedule are fixed. They also prove calibration acquisition-cost separations between interrogation protocols. Their controlled-dilation formulation is broad: describing an unknown parameter as part of a Hamiltonian instead of an environment does not distinguish the present problem. The relevant distinction is the optimization over the encoding as well as the recovery, together with the scaling number of unknown spatial parameters and a classical information budget.

[Deng (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30910) already treats calibration of a static spatial sign in Gaussian correlated noise. For a fixed nine-qubit code, fixed checks and recovery, and a fixed control menu, the preparation used in calibration changes its acquisition cost and can change task feasibility. Thus static spatial calibration itself is not a new topic. The present information frontier permits arbitrary compressed profile records and arbitrary endpoint codes on a growing register. It prices the information retained about the profile, not the experiments required to acquire that information.

These distinctions also delimit the current result. It is not an optimal sensing protocol, a sample-complexity theorem, or a bound for arbitrary active error correction during storage. Independent endpoint randomness and in-register probes are allowed; intermediate controls and a retained external quantum calibration program are outside the model.

## Classical information that reverses noise

[Buscemi, Chiribella, and D'Ariano (2005)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.95.090501) analyze inversion of decoherence by measuring the environment and applying classical feedback. For a random-unitary realization they bound the entropy of the correction label below by the entropy exchange at the maximally mixed input; for qubit decoherence the minimum equals that entropy exchange. The information-cost interpretation of reversing a phase error is therefore established. Their record describes a realized error in a run and enables reversal of the full input. Here a pre-storage record describes the static generator, can select the encoded subspace, and remains useful for every later common phase without observing that phase.

This distinction does not make the finite exact bound a new general information inequality. At a particular common phase, the two-valued profile acts as an independent Pauli phase key up to a fixed unitary. The bounds $D\leq M$ and $\log_2 D\leq I(S:C\mid K)$ then have the familiar interpretation that classical key information is required to recover entanglement. Their proof uses standard relative-entropy data processing and a maximally entangled-state test. Pair-parity advice provides an exact finite-size construction in the appropriate rate range.

[Ilin and Pereg (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.23459) study a closely related rate-limited helper resource in noisy quantum relay channels. Their primary abstract states that a relay observes information about the Pauli errors that occur, compresses the error sequence, and sends a bin index over a classical link to the receiver; matching capacity bounds are obtained for two models. Only that primary abstract was available for this comparison. No particular capacity formula or full-theorem subsumption claim is attributed to the paper here. Its realized-error record differs from reusable static-profile advice available before encoding, but a linear information/rate slope alone is not a sufficient distinction.

[Warsi and Coon (2017)](https://doi.org/10.1109/TIT.2017.2678520) give an information-spectrum treatment of rate-limited channel-state information at the encoder. Their theorems allow general channel and state sequences, so memorylessness is not a valid basis for excluding that framework. Their transmitted message is classical and the channel is classical-quantum. The present payload is entanglement, and the static-profile record is available to both endpoint operations. The general idea of pricing channel knowledge is inherited.

## Channel uncertainty, mixtures, and outage

[Bjelaković, Boche, and Nötzel (2008)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.78.042331) derive capacities for compound quantum channels and, in Corollary IV.7, finite averaged channels of the form $\sum_i p_i\mathcal N_i^{\otimes n}$. Their [universal coding work](https://arxiv.org/abs/0811.4588) treats entanglement transmission and generation under channel uncertainty. Mixtures with a channel parameter held fixed across a block are therefore already part of quantum capacity theory. [Buscemi and Datta (2010)](https://doi.org/10.1109/TIT.2009.2039166) give one-shot bounds and information-spectrum capacity formulas for channel sequences with arbitrary correlations. The present model does not require a new general capacity framework.

Indeed, in the signed model $g_0=0$, random local $X$ conjugations make the uncalibrated minimax problem equivalent to the exchangeable mixture

$$
\mathbb E_{\theta}\bigl[\mathcal D_{\cos(2\delta\theta)}^{\otimes n}\bigr].
$$

Its support contains channels arbitrarily close to complete dephasing. A fixed positive probability of such unfavorable phases obstructs vanishing error at positive rate. This is an outage mechanism. [Etxezarreta Martinez et al. (2022)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.105.012432) explicitly discuss quantum outage for decoherence parameters that fluctuate between blocks while remaining fixed within a block. Their work supplies useful operational context; the finite fidelity bounds here are proved directly and do not rely on a general outage formula from that paper.

The binary result is consequently not a strong converse asserting that fidelity tends to zero above the information frontier. At weak fixed noise its obstructing Gaussian tail may be very small. The separate continuum theorem addresses fixed error: uncertainty over an interval produces a sinc attenuation under the converse prior, leading to a lifetime of order $\delta^{-2}$ at every fixed rate below one. Increasing register size cannot remove that obstruction within the stated passive model.

## Scope of the resulting contribution

The contribution is a pair of explicit resource theorems for passive storage with unknown spatial couplings. They allow arbitrary endpoint codes, stochastic or compressed classical calibration records, free independent classical keys, and probes counted within the same noisy register. The fixed-error lifetime bound and its achievable order show that a low-rank common field does not by itself guarantee scalable accessible protection. The two-valued subfamily gives an exact information frontier, with matching decoherence-free constructions.

The Schur comparison, entanglement-breaking overlap bound, minimax averaging, relative-entropy data processing, protected-subspace construction, and outage interpretation are inherited or standard ingredients. The results do not determine a sharp calibration-information tradeoff for the full continuum family, acquisition complexity, or an active-control optimum. Those distinctions are part of the mathematical scope, not assumptions that can be dropped when interpreting the conclusions.
